CBG-MEB Clarifies QR Code Rules on Pharmaceutical Packaging

The Dutch Medicines Evaluation Board (CBG-MEB) has issued guidance stating that QR codes on pharmaceutical packaging or package leaflets must not contradict the approved Summary of Product Characteristics, must be useful for the patient, and may not be used to promote the medicinal product. Direct implication for QR-driven patient engagement strategies.

📈 Trends to Watch

1. AI Agents Replace AI Tools in Commercial Operations

The industry is crossing a threshold: AI is no longer augmenting human workflows — it is running them end-to-end. Platforms like Daily Command and PharmaForceIQ%AKtana signal that brand teams will increasingly operate through AI-driven decision systems. For smaller pharma businesses without in-house marketing, this democratises access to sophisticated commercial capabilities previously out of reach.

2. The Compliance Tightening Cycle — EU and US Moving in Parallel

From EFPIA's monthly Code modules and ePI rollout in Europe, to the FDA's enforcement blitz and proposed influencer legislation in the US, regulatory pressure on pharma marketing is intensifying on both sides of the Atlantic. The CBG-MEB's QR code restrictions are a local Dutch expression of this global trend. Compliance-first marketing is becoming a competitive differentiator, not just a legal obligation.

3. The DTP Shift: When Marketing Becomes a Service Channel

The rise of direct-to-patient programmes signals a fundamental rethink of the pharma marketing funnel. As brands create self-pay patient access pathways, the role of the pharma marketer is expanding into patient experience design. For smaller healthcare businesses, this opens an opportunity to differentiate through genuine patient-centricity rather than promotional spend alone.
